Recirculating 12V DEVap System for High Humidity RVs

Detailed Design Canvas (External Packaged Unit)

I. System Identity & Core Technology

System Name: External Packaged 12V DEVap RV Cooling System

Objective: Low-power (<50W electrical draw), high-efficiency cooling for humid climates, zero interior footprint, minimizing resource consumption.

Key Innovation: A fully external, hitch-mounted packaged unit. It uses a dual-duct closed-loop system to recirculate cabin air, drastically increasing desiccant longevity while keeping all mechanical noise, water, and heavy silica gel cartridges entirely outside the living space.

Overall Concept: RV Cabin Return Duct ➔ External Unit Intake ➔ Step 1 (Desiccant: Hot & Dry) ➔ Step 2 (Heat Exchanger: Cooled & Dry) ➔ Step 3 (Swamp Cooler: Cold & Humid) ➔ External Unit Exhaust ➔ RV Cabin Supply Duct.

II. Component Level Detailed Design & Selection (12V DC)

1. Air Moving Systems

Main Process Blower (Step 1-3): 12V Centrifugal blower fan (approx. 20W-30W max draw). Needs exceptional static pressure capability (2+ inch water gauge) to pull air from the RV, push it through the thick desiccant bed, the heat exchanger, the wet pad, and back through the return duct. Target airflow: ~150 CFM.

Secondary Heat Exchanger Fan (Step 2): 12V High-volume axial fan (approx. 10W draw). Target airflow: 300+ CFM. Pulls ambient outside air strictly for sensible cooling of the heat exchanger core.

2. Desiccant Cartridges (Step 1)

Materials & Construction: Shallow, wide rectangular cartridges fabricated from lightweight structural aluminum. Wire mesh (100 mesh stainless steel) on intake/exhaust faces.

Silica Gel Specs: 12V Indicating Silica Gel (changes color when saturated).

Quantity: 15-20 lbs per cartridge. Because the unit is mounted outside (e.g., on a hitch carrier), swapping heavy cartridges is physically much easier than an interior build.

3. Air-to-Air Heat Exchanger (Step 2)

Core Specifications: Cross-flow aluminum heat exchanger core (e.g., stacked corrugated plates). Suggested dimensions: 10" x 10" x 10" core.

Integration: Must be sealed perfectly to prevent the process air (RV loop) from mixing with the secondary cooling air (Outside loop).

4. Swamp Cooler Module (Step 3)

Module Construction: Integrated into the external housing.

Evaporative Pad Specs: High-efficiency honeycomb structured cellulose evaporative media. Thickness: 4 inches.

Water System: Submersible 12V DC water pump (10W, ~150 GPH flow rate). Integrated water reservoir at the base of the external unit.

III. System Integration & Layout Design

1. External Housing & Mounting

Unit Placement: Mounted on a standard 2-inch hitch cargo carrier at the rear of the RV. This supports the heavy weight (water + 60lbs of silica gel + components) safely.

Weatherproofing: The entire assembly must be enclosed in a weatherproof, UV-resistant housing (e.g., fabricated aluminum, marine starboard, or a modified heavy-duty truck toolbox). It must feature baffled intake/exhaust vents for the Step 2 cooling fan to prevent rain intrusion.

2. RV Connection & Ducting (The Dual-Pipe System)

Duct Material: R-6 or R-8 insulated flexible HVAC ducting (4-inch or 6-inch diameter). Extreme insulation is mandatory to prevent heat gain as the cold air travels outside.

RV Penetration Methods:

Temporary/Testing: A rigid window insert panel with two 4-inch flanges. Allows for quick setup without permanent RV modification.

Permanent (Recommended): Marine deck plates (waterproof screw-in inspection hatches) installed through the RV floor or lower side wall. Ducts attach via twist-lock collars. Caps are screwed on when the unit is disconnected.

Airflow Path: Pull warm air from high up in the RV (if using window) or low (if using floor) ➔ heavily insulated return duct ➔ External Packaged Unit ➔ heavily insulated supply duct ➔ blast cold air into the cabin.

3. Control System & Electrical Design

Control Scheme: A multi-core 12V wire bundle (e.g., 5-wire trailer cable) runs alongside the ducting to bring a control switch panel inside the RV. This allows the user to turn on the main blower, the heat exchanger fan, and the water pump independently from the comfort of the cabin.

Electrical Specs: Total estimated maximum current draw is ~40W-50W (3.5 to 4.5 Amps at 12V).

Power Source: Plugged into a dedicated 12V exterior receptacle (e.g., a standard 12V cigarette lighter plug or Anderson Powerpole connector mounted near the hitch).

IV. Operational & Maintenance Plan

1. Cartridge Regeneration (Solar Thermal Oven)

Solar Oven Design: Large, well-insulated box painted matte black inside, with a clear tempered glass top. Because the system is external, the solar oven can be mounted directly next to the AC unit on the hitch carrier or deployed on the ground nearby. Target internal temp: 220°F+.

Swap Procedure: Open the weatherproof access door on the AC unit, slide out the saturated cartridge, place it in the solar oven, and slide in a dry cartridge.

2. General Maintenance

Drainage: The external unit requires a simple petcock valve on the bottom of the water reservoir to easily drain it onto the ground before driving or storing.

Vibration: Because the unit is riding on the hitch, all internal components (fans, heat exchanger core) must be mounted with rubber isolation dampers to survive road vibration.

V. Design Optimization & Constraints

1. Buffered System Design for Nighttime Operation

To run the system at night (when the solar oven doesn't work), you must store "buffered" (extra) cartridges.

If swapping every 3 hours (extended due to cabin recirculation), an 8-hour night requires at least 3 pre-baked, fully dried, and sealed cartridges ready to go before the sun goes down.

Advantage of External Build: A sealed storage compartment for dry cartridges can be built directly into the hitch carrier setup, freeing up valuable interior RV cabinet space.

2. Security

The entire unit, along with the solar oven and spare cartridges, must be locked securely to the hitch carrier to prevent theft while boondocking or parked in public spaces.
